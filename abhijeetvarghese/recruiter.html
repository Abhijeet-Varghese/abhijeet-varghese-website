02Before you read my CV

There is a useful distinction.

A CV tells you where I have been.

A portfolio tells you what I have made.

Neither completely explains how I work.

My career has increasingly moved toward problems that sit between disciplines.

Problems where a creative idea has to work with technology. Where technology has to serve a human experience. Where content has to work inside a physical environment. Where a brand has to become something people can actually experience. Where multiple teams need to work toward one outcome. And where the person leading the creative thinking also needs to understand what happens when the idea enters production.

That space between disciplines is where I do my best work.

03I don't start with the medium

I don't start with the medium.

A common question in experience work is: “Should we use VR?”

I think the better question is: “What does the audience need to experience?”

Then: “What is the best way to create that experience?”

Sometimes the answer is spatial. Sometimes digital. Sometimes interactive. Sometimes immersive. Sometimes a combination. Sometimes the simplest solution is the strongest one.

I am interested in technology that earns its place. AR, VR, XR, interactive systems, AI and emerging technologies are powerful tools. But the technology is never the idea.

The human experience is the idea.

05How I work

Seven stages. One intent.

01

Understand

I start by understanding the problem rather than immediately designing the answer.

  • What is happening?
  • Why does it matter?
  • Who is involved?
  • What needs to change?
  • What does success actually mean?
02

Find the opportunity

The brief gives you requirements. The interesting idea is often hiding underneath them. I look for the opportunity that can make the experience more useful, meaningful, memorable or effective.

03

Build the idea

I develop the creative territory, experience logic and narrative.

  • What is the central idea?
  • How does it unfold?
  • What does the audience encounter first?
  • What happens next?
  • What holds everything together?
04

Design the experience

This is where the idea becomes a journey. Spatial. Digital. Interactive. Immersive. Visual. Auditory. Physical. The medium changes. The thinking doesn't.

05

Bring technology in

Technology becomes part of the experience architecture. Not decoration. Not a checkbox. Not something added at the end. It has to have a reason to exist.

06

Align the people

Creative work is made by people. Designers. Developers. Artists. Technologists. Content teams. Production teams. Clients. Stakeholders. Vendors. Partners. The experience only becomes coherent when those people are moving toward the same thing.

07

Stay with the work

I don't believe creative leadership ends when the concept is approved. That is when the idea meets reality. Technical constraints. Budgets. Timelines. Fabrication. Content revisions. Client feedback. Production decisions. Unexpected problems. The job is to protect the intent while finding a way forward.

02

Indian Army Immersive Training

When immersive technology has a real job to do.

The Indian Army immersive training project represents another side of my work. Here, VR isn't being used simply because immersive technology is impressive. It is part of a larger training system.

A dedicated training room was established for Army personnel with 15 high-end computers and a high-end trainer display that allows the trainer to monitor what trainees are doing inside their VR headsets. The immersive platform also incorporates qualification and testing inside the VR environment.

The training model introduces a progression gate:

  • Immersive training
  • Qualification
  • Demonstrated performance
  • Progression to physical / battlefield-based training
Read the full story

That changes the role of immersive technology. It becomes part of the learning and qualification process. The experience therefore has to work for more than one person. It has to work for the trainee. It has to work for the trainer. It has to support learning. It has to support assessment. And it has to connect the digital environment to the physical training that follows.

For me, this is what makes immersive design interesting: not putting someone inside a virtual world, but designing what that virtual world is supposed to accomplish.

07My creative principles

Rules inside a creative operating system.

01

Start with people.

Before the technology. Before the interface. Before the visual. Who is experiencing this? What do they need? What are they thinking? What should happen next?

02

The medium serves the idea.

I don't choose technology because it is available. I choose it when it creates something the experience genuinely needs.

03

Content is part of design.

Words, images, sound, motion, narration and interaction are not separate from the experience. They are the experience.

04

Complexity needs clarity.

The more complicated the subject, the more carefully the experience has to be designed. Good design doesn't hide complexity. It makes complexity understandable.

05

Everything should belong to one idea.

The room. The screen. The interaction. The content. The technology. The sound. The interface. The physical journey. They should feel like different parts of the same thought.

06

Execution is creative work.

The final 20% can change the quality of the entire experience. That is why I stay involved.

07

Make technology human.

Technology is powerful when people understand what to do with it. The goal isn't to impress someone with technology. The goal is to make technology disappear into the experience.

07Leadership

I work with people as much as I work with ideas.

The work is multidisciplinary by nature. That means leadership isn't simply assigning tasks. It is creating a shared understanding of what everyone is building.

I work across conversations between:

  • Creative ↔ Client
  • Strategy ↔ Design
  • Design ↔ Technology
  • Content ↔ Experience
  • Concept ↔ Production
  • Vision ↔ Reality

Sometimes the most important contribution isn't creating another asset. It is getting five people to agree on what the experience is actually trying to achieve.

07Enterprise work changed how I think

Enterprise work changed how I think.

Enterprise projects are rarely just creative exercises. They involve: multiple stakeholders, complex information, business objectives, operational realities, technology, content, physical environments, approval structures, production, delivery.

That complexity has shaped the way I approach creative work. I have learned to hold two things at the same time: creative ambition and executional reality. Neither should cancel the other.

Where I see the next evolution.

The boundaries between creative disciplines are disappearing. Experience is becoming increasingly: physical + digital, human + machine, brand + environment, content + interaction, design + technology, creative + AI.

This is the direction I am interested in. Not technology for its own sake. Not AI for its own sake. But what happens when new tools allow creative teams to think, prototype, communicate and produce differently.

I am particularly interested in the emerging relationship between: Experience Design, Enterprise Innovation, Creative Technology, AI-Enabled Creative Production, Human Understanding.

That intersection is where I want to keep building.

AI is a tool. The thinking still matters.

AI is changing creative production. It can accelerate exploration. Expand possibilities. Help prototype ideas. Generate variations. Support production. Change how teams work. But faster production does not automatically mean better creative work.

The real advantage comes from knowing: what to make, why to make it, what to reject, what to refine, and how to connect the result back to the human problem.

That is where creative judgment becomes even more important.

I see AI as an extension of the creative system—not a replacement for thinking.
