02 / THE SHIFT

I didn't start out
designing experiences.

I started in VFX and animation. That taught me to think through frames, movement, composition, timing and visual storytelling.

Then the frame became interaction. Interaction became environment. Environment became the whole experience.

Today, I don't see creative direction, experience design, content, spatial design and technology as separate disciplines. They are different tools for solving the same problem: creating something meaningful for a person to encounter.

03 / WHAT I ACTUALLY DO

I move between
the strategic, creative and practical.

I can start with a blank page and help define the experience. I can take an existing brief and find the larger creative opportunity inside it. I can build the story, shape the interaction, work through the content, define the role of technology and coordinate the people who have to build it.

And I can stay close enough to execution to know when the beautiful idea on paper is not going to work in reality.

04 / MY WAY
01

I start with the problem.

Not the deliverable. Not the software. Not the technology. I want to understand what is actually being asked, why it matters, who it is for and what should change after the experience exists.

02

I look for the idea inside the brief.

A brief is usually written in the language of requirements. The creative opportunity is often hiding underneath it. I look for the thing that could make people stop, understand, feel or remember.

03

I build the story before decorating the experience.

Audience, sequence, context, interaction, emotion, information and memory come first. The visual layer should grow from that thinking.

04

I use technology when it earns its place.

AR, VR, XR, interactive systems, digital interfaces and emerging technology are tools. I am interested in what they allow a person to experience that another medium cannot.

05

I stay close to execution.

Creative leadership does not stop when the concept is approved. Ideas become real through hundreds of decisions — technical, spatial, content, production and human.

06

I make complexity easier for teams.

Creative, design, technology, content, architecture, production, clients and stakeholders often see different parts of the problem. I work to keep the whole experience coherent.

06 / WHAT I HAVE LEARNED

People don't experience your org chart.

They experience one thing. They don't care which team created the content, which vendor fabricated the environment or which designer created the interface. They experience the whole.

More technology does not automatically create more impact.

The best technology is often the technology the audience stops noticing. What matters is what it enables.

A beautiful experience can still fail.

If people don't understand what to do, if the story is confusing or if the technology gets in the way, visual polish cannot rescue the experience.

Execution is part of creativity.

A concept that cannot be built is incomplete. Constraints are not always the enemy. Sometimes they force the better idea.

07 / HOW I LEAD

I don't believe creative leadership is about having the loudest voice in the room.

It is about creating clarity.

People need to understand what we are trying to achieve, why we are doing it, what good looks like, what matters most, what can change and what cannot.

I like bringing different perspectives into the room. Then I like making decisions.

My approach is collaborative, but not directionless.
