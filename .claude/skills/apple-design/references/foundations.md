# Apple Design Foundations

Official index: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/foundations

## Layout

- Group related items using space, alignment, separators, color, or material while keeping controls distinct from content.
- Give essential information enough room and progressively disclose secondary detail.
- Extend backgrounds/content to screen or window edges; navigation and controls float in their own functional layer.
- Follow reading order, adapt to RTL, and preserve semantic hierarchy.
- Adapt to screen/window size, orientation, external displays, safe areas, Dynamic Type, localization, and input changes.

Official: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout

## Color and appearance

- Prefer semantic system colors because they adapt to appearance and accessibility settings.
- Never encode meaning in color alone.
- Validate light/dark appearances, increased contrast, transparency reduction, and different displays.
- Use accent color to communicate interaction/selection, not as indiscriminate decoration.

Official: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color

## Typography

- Start with San Francisco and Dynamic Type/system text styles on Apple platforms.
- Preserve text hierarchy under enlargement; don’t lock text into fixed-height boxes.
- Use weight, size, spacing, and placement deliberately; avoid ultra-thin small text.
- Test long localization, RTL, accessibility sizes, and text spacing.

Official: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography

## Materials

- Materials communicate hierarchy and retain a sense of place.
- Liquid Glass belongs primarily to the controls/navigation layer.
- Standard materials belong within the content layer.
- Choose materials semantically, not because of an apparent fixed color.
- Provide solid/high-contrast fallbacks for Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast.

Official: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials

## Motion

- Motion must provide feedback, continuity, state, or instruction—not exist for spectacle.
- Keep feedback brief and precise.
- Match direction and physics to the initiating gesture.
- Make motion optional, cancellable, and interruptible.
- Avoid repeatedly animating frequent UI operations.
- visionOS motion needs special comfort safeguards and a stable reference.

Official: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion

## Icons and SF Symbols

- Prefer SF Symbols for standard interface meanings.
- Match symbol weight/scale to nearby text.
- Choose monochrome, hierarchical, palette, or multicolor rendering based on semantics.
- Confirm OS availability for each symbol/effect.
- Don’t use SF Symbols in app icons, logos, or trademark-like roles.

Official: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sf-symbols

## Privacy and responsibility

- Ask only for data required for a clear user benefit.
- Explain permission requests in context and at the moment of need.
- Make data use and consequences transparent.
- Provide safe recovery, undo, and clear destructive-action boundaries.

Official: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy
